How it works

The Schulte table, made fun.

A Schulte table is a grid of shuffled numbers that you find in order, as quickly as you can. Named after the German psychiatrist Walter Schulte, it has been used for decades to practise attention, visual search and speed reading.

The trick is to stop hunting number by number. Fix your eyes near the center and let the next number come to you. The more you play, the wider your field of attention feels, and the faster your times get. 49 adds what the paper version never had: modes, scores, streaks and a record of every game.

  1. Look at the centerKeep your gaze steady and use your side vision.
  2. Find the next numberTap it. A miss costs you time, so stay calm.
  3. Beat yesterdayYour time, your pace and your misses show you how you're improving.

Styles

Pure focus, or chase the score.

Every mode can be played in three styles, and each style keeps its own records and ranks.

◎ Normal

A clean time trial.

Just you, the board and the clock. No effects, no points, nothing to pull your eyes away from the numbers. This is the classic Schulte table, and the purest test of your focus.

  • Your time, pace and misses

🔥 Competitive

Points, combos, ×3.

Every number is worth 100 points, plus a speed bonus of up to 50 that shrinks the longer you take to find it. Find the next one before the combo timer runs out and your multiplier climbs.

Multiplayer

Same board. Who's faster?

Play 1 v 1, 2 v 2 or with a whole group, from a class to a team at work. Everyone gets exactly the same board, so the only difference is focus. Start a room, share its code, QR code or link, and pick a nickname for the game.

Race

Same board for everyone, round after round. The fastest wins each round, and points add up to the final standings.

Points battle

Ranked by Competitive points, so speed and combos both count. A clean, fast streak can beat a quicker but messy run.

Knockout

For groups: the slowest player each round is out, until one is left standing.

Ghost race

Play a board, then friends race your run whenever they like that day, with a live "ahead or behind" as they play.

Steal

One board, live, head to head. Whoever taps a number first scores it, and it disappears for the other player.

Easy to join

A four-character room code, a QR code to scan or a link to tap. Your own nickname for each game, since Game Center names are often random.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

When does 49 come out?

Soon. 49 is in open beta now: join it on TestFlight with the button above. The App Store for iPhone comes next.

What's the difference between Normal and Competitive?

Normal is a plain time trial: no effects and no points, just your time. Competitive adds points, combos and a multiplier up to ×3, with effects and sounds that build as you go. Each style has its own records and ranks.

How does scoring work in Competitive?

Each number is worth 100 points plus a speed bonus of up to 50 that shrinks the longer you take. A combo of 5 doubles your points and a combo of 15 triples them. If the combo timer runs out the combo is lost, and a wrong tap halves it. Numbers slowly lose value as a game goes on, so studying the board before you start never pays.

Is it really free?

Yes. Every mode, board, look and feature is free. Free players see an ad between some games, never during one. Premium removes the ads, and you can also watch one ad for 30 minutes without any.

Can I play with friends?

Yes. Multiplayer has 1 v 1, 2 v 2 and group rooms with Race, Points battle, Knockout and Ghost race. Everyone plays the same board; friends join with a code, a QR code or a link. You can also pass your phone around in guest mode.

What do I need to play?

An iPhone with iOS 17 or later.

What is a Schulte table?

A grid of shuffled numbers that you find in order as fast as you can. It's a classic exercise for attention, visual search and speed reading, named after the psychiatrist Walter Schulte.

Will it improve my focus?

You'll see your own times, pace and accuracy improve as you play, and Insights shows you where. 49 is a game for practising focus, not a medical product: it doesn't diagnose, treat or prevent any condition.

Which languages does it speak?

English, Polish, German, French, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, Italian, Japanese and Korean.

Does it collect my data?

Your games, scores and stats stay on your iPhone, and there's no account. To improve the game, the app also sends anonymous usage statistics (no names, no game history), which you can turn off in Settings. Game Center is optional. The ads in the free version come from an ad network, which may collect some data to show them; Premium removes them. See the privacy policy for details.

How do I restore Premium on a new iPhone?

Open Settings → Premium in the app and tap Restore purchases, signed in with the same Apple Account you bought it with.

Is there an Android version?

Not at the moment. 49 is made for iPhone.
