The drill

What is a Schulte table?

A Schulte table is a grid of shuffled numbers that you find in order, as fast as you can. It's named after Walter Schulte, a German psychiatrist, and has long been used as an exercise for attention, visual search and speed reading. The classic table is 5 × 5, with the numbers 1 to 25.

How to play

  1. Pick a board. 5 × 5 is the classic; 7 × 7 is the standard in 49.
  2. Tap 1, then 2, then 3, all the way to the last number. The clock starts on your first tap.
  3. A wrong tap counts as a miss but doesn't stop the clock, so keep going.
  4. For a change, count down: start from the highest number.

Tips

How to get faster

01

Keep your eyes near the center of the board and let your side vision find the next number, instead of reading row by row.

02

Say the next number in your head before you look for it.

03

Don't rush after a miss. A calm second is faster than three wrong taps.

04

Play a little every day. Short, regular practice beats one long session.

FAQ

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. The board on this page is free, with no account and no ads. The 49 app is free to play too.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes, on phones, tablets and computers. On a computer you can also use the keyboard: move to a number with Tab and press Enter.

Does a Schulte table improve focus?

Your times improve with practice, and the exercise works the attention and visual search it relies on. It's a game for practising focus, not a medical treatment.

What's a good time?

It depends on the board and on you, so compare with your own best, which this page remembers. In the 49 app, Game Center leaderboards show how you compare with other players.
