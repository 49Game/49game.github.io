Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Who we are

49 - Focus Booster ("49") is made by Artur Pereć in Poland, who is responsible for your data under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). You can reach us at contact49game@icloud.com.

49 is a game for iPhone and for Android phones. The two apps are the same game, but they run on different services: Apple's iCloud and Game Center on iPhone, Google's Firebase on Android. Where they differ, the sections below say which app they mean.

Your games, on your iPhone and in your iCloud

Your games, scores, statistics, achievements and settings are stored on your iPhone. If you are signed in to iCloud, your games, achievements and streak freezes are also copied to your own private iCloud storage, so they come back on a new iPhone or after you reinstall the app. Apple keeps that copy in your iCloud account, and we can't see it. Your settings, look and purchases are not part of it. There is no account to create, and your game history is not sent to us.

Deleting the app deletes the data on your iPhone. To delete the iCloud copy, open your iPhone's Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage → 49, and delete its data. The copy exists to give you the feature you use, so we rely on Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract).

Your games, on your Android phone

On Android, your games, scores, statistics, achievements and settings are stored on your phone. If backup is on in your phone's settings, Android also copies them to your own Google account, so they come back when you set up a new phone with that account or install 49 again. Google keeps that copy in your Google account, and we can't see it. Your multiplayer profile, friends and conversations are not part of it: to take them to a new phone, link your profile to Google, as described below. There is no account to create, and your game history is not sent to us.

Deleting the app deletes the data on your phone. You can turn backup off in your phone's settings, and delete a phone's backup in Google Drive, under Backups. As on iPhone, the copy exists to give you the feature you use, so we rely on Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract).

Multiplayer on iPhone

On iPhone, multiplayer runs on Apple's iCloud, in a public database that every copy of 49 for iPhone shares. When you open Multiplayer, 49 creates a player profile there with your nickname, your Game Center photo if you have one, your multiplayer ratings, your record and your recent games (when, which game, your place, your opponents and how your rating changed). When you play in a room or a game of Steal, the room's settings, its name if it has one, every player's nickname and each round's results (times, points, mistakes and splits) are stored there too. So are friend requests, accepted requests and invites to games, and chat messages: the ones players send each other after a multiplayer game and the ones friends send each other (ready-made phrases, emoji, or text you type, which only friends can send each other). While 49 is open, about once a minute, your profile also notes when you were last seen, so other players see a green dot by your name while you're online. You can turn that off in Settings → Account Settings → Show when I'm online. A room's password is never stored: only a scrambled value (a hash) made from it and the room's code, which can check a password but not reveal it. Rooms made with versions of 49 before 1.0 (21) stored the password itself.

Other players of 49 can see your nickname, photo, ratings, recent games and whether you're online, the players in your rooms see your results and the chat after the game, and your friends see the messages you send them. Choose a nickname you're happy for others to see. We can see this data as well, and we use it only to run multiplayer. Your profile is linked to your Game Center player ID (an identifier Apple makes for each game) when you are signed in to Game Center, otherwise to a random ID made on your iPhone. It is not linked to your name, email address or Apple Account. To host or join a room you need to be signed in to iCloud. Games in guest mode are never stored there.

Typed messages pass a word filter on your iPhone before they're sent. If you report a message, it's hidden for you at once and saved in iCloud with your player ID and the sender's, for us to review. We keep a report only as long as we need it to look into it and act on it, for example to remove a player who breaks the rules. Blocking a player is stored only on your iPhone. Reviewing reports keeps the chat safe for everyone, which is our legitimate interest under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.

When a room closes, your entries in it are removed. Your profile and friend messages stay so that your ratings and friends keep working, until you delete them. To delete them, open Settings, tap Account Settings and tap Delete my multiplayer profile (in test versions before 1.0 (30) it was under User Profile, and before 1.0 (26) in Multiplayer, on your profile). That removes from iCloud your profile and photo, the friend requests, invites and messages you sent, and your entries and chat messages in rooms, and removes your name from the rooms you hosted. On your iPhone it clears your nickname, friends, conversations and room history. iCloud only lets the player who created a record delete it, so messages other players sent you, and your nickname in other players' recent games, stay until you email us and we remove them. You can also email us to delete everything at once. You can play every other part of 49 without opening Multiplayer. We rely on Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract), because this is the feature you choose to use.

Multiplayer and leaderboards on Android

On Android, multiplayer, friends, chat and leaderboards run on Google's Firebase, which handles the data for us as our processor. To use them, you sign in with Google Play Games. If your phone is already signed in to Play Games, 49 signs you in when it starts, without asking. Otherwise the online screens offer it, and Google shows its own sign-in. Firebase then keeps your Play Games player ID with your sign-in, together with the name and photo address Google passes along. 49's own records store your Play Games gamer tag, as described below, but no Play Games ID or photo. Without Play Games you can play everything else, and nothing is sent to Firebase: the scores you set wait on your phone and are posted once you sign in. Versions of 49 for Android before 0.5 signed in to Firebase anonymously, with a random ID and no name, email address or password. Profiles made that way keep working, and signing in to Play Games links it to the same profile. To prevent abuse, Firebase notes the IP address of each sign-in and keeps it for a few weeks. 49's records are stored in Google's data centres in Warsaw, Poland, and live Steal matches in Belgium, so they stay in the EU. The sign-in itself runs in Google's data centres in the United States.

Once you're signed in, opening Multiplayer or picking a nickname for the leaderboards creates a player profile there with your Play Games gamer tag and the nickname you pick (until you pick one, or if you clear it, your gamer tag is your nickname), your multiplayer ratings, your record, your recent games (when, which game, your place, your opponents and how your rating changed), the games you left early in the last day (so 49 can warn players who often leave) and your top 3 places in the Daily Challenge and on the leaderboards. When you play in a room, the room's settings, its name if it has one, every player's nickname and each round's results (times, points, mistakes and splits) are stored there too. So are friend requests, accepted requests and invites to games, and chat messages: the ones players send each other after a multiplayer game and the ones friends send each other (ready-made phrases, emoji, or text you type, which only friends can send each other). While 49 is open, about once a minute, your profile also notes when you were last seen, so other players see a green dot by your name while you're online. You can turn that off in Settings → Account Settings → Show when I'm online. As on iPhone, a room's password is never stored, only a scrambled value (a hash) made from it.

Steal, the live game where players race for the same numbers, runs on Firebase's Realtime Database, in Google's data centres in Belgium, so it stays in the EU. While a Steal match lasts, and only then, it holds each player's nickname and multiplayer ID, whether they play ranked and whether they're still connected, every number claimed and the chat after the board. When your phone leaves the match, your place in it and its messages are deleted, and the last phone to leave deletes the match. While you search for an opponent, the search holds your sign-in ID and the time, until a match starts or for at most 15 minutes. Only the players in a match can read its numbers and chat. Your Steal rating and recent Steal games are kept in your profile, like your other games.

To join a room by its QR code, 49 uses Google's code scanner on your phone. Google collects technical diagnostics and an identifier for this installation from the scanner, to keep it working. See Google's ML Kit terms.

49 on Android has its own leaderboards in place of Game Center. Your entry on each board holds your gamer tag and nickname and your best score of the day, the 7 days, the month and all time, with when you set each one. 49 also reads the boards to tell you when someone takes your top 3 place and to find your place in the Daily Challenge.

Other players of 49 on Android can see your nickname, ratings, recent games, top 3 places, leaderboard scores and whether you're online, the players in your rooms see your results and the chat after the game, and your friends see the messages you send them. Rooms, their results and their chat are stored where any copy of 49 on Android can read them, and 49 shows them only to the players in that room. Choose a nickname you're happy for others to see. We can see this data as well, and we use it only to run multiplayer and the leaderboards. Your profile is linked to the random ID of your Firebase sign-in, and through it to your Play Games account, so it comes back on a new phone. Other players see your gamer tag beside your nickname, on your profile and on the leaderboards, as iPhone players see Game Center names. They never see your Play Games ID or your Google account. For now, players on Android and on iPhone don't meet: each app has its own players, friends and leaderboards.

Before version 0.5, Settings → Account Settings offered Link to Google. Profiles linked that way stay linked, and Play Games is added when you sign in to it. For those, Google shared your Google account's ID, email address, name and profile photo with Firebase, which keeps them with your sign-in so you can sign back in. They are not added to your profile, and other players never see them.

Typed messages pass a word filter on your phone before they're sent. If you report a message, it's hidden for you at once and saved in Firebase with your player ID, the sender's and where it was said, for us to review. We keep reports and rely on the same legitimate interest as on iPhone, described above. Blocking a player is stored only on your phone.

Deleting your multiplayer profile on Android

The next time you open 49 after a room closes, your phone removes your entries in it. Your profile, leaderboard entries and friend messages stay so that your ratings, places and friends keep working, until you delete them. To delete them in 49 - Focus Booster on Android, open Settings, tap Account Settings and tap Delete my multiplayer profile. That removes from Firebase your profile, your leaderboard entries, your push token, the friend requests, invites and messages you sent, and your entries and chat messages in rooms, and removes your name from the rooms you hosted. It also deletes your sign-in, with its link to your Play Games account, and to your Google account if you linked one. 49 first checks your Play Games account again, and asks you to confirm your Google account if you linked one. Your Play Games profile itself belongs to Google and stays. If your sign-in still can't be deleted, 49 tells you so, and we delete it when you email us. On your phone it clears your nickname, friends, conversations and room history. Google then removes the deleted data from its backups within 180 days.

Firebase only lets the player who wrote a record delete it, so messages other players sent you, your nickname in other players' recent games and in the list of players of other players' rooms, and reports you made or that name you, stay until you email us and we remove them. We delete reports once we've reviewed them. You can also email us at contact49game@icloud.com to delete everything at once, even without the app. Tell us your nickname, so we can find your records. You can play every other part of 49 without opening Multiplayer. We rely on Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract), because this is the feature you choose to use.

Your skills

From version 1.0 (39), 49 works out seven skills from your saved games (Speed, Focus, Accuracy, Rhythm, Field of view, Stamina and Flexibility) and a 49 Score. You choose who can see them under "Who can see your skills" on your profile: Everyone, Friends or Only me. Everyone is the starting choice, and you can change it at any time.

Under Everyone and Friends, your multiplayer profile holds a short summary: your 49 Score, the seven skill scores, how they changed in the last 7 days and your 49 Score on each of the last 30 days you played. If you're signed in to Game Center and have no profile yet, 49 creates one for it, with your nickname and Game Center name, as described under Multiplayer on iPhone. If you're not signed in to Game Center and have never opened Multiplayer, your skills stay on your iPhone. Under Everyone, any player who opens your profile in 49 sees your skills. Under Friends, 49 shows them only to your friends. The summary is still stored with the rest of your profile in the public database, so pick Only me if you want it to stay on your devices. Under Only me, your profile keeps only your choice and when you made it, so your other iPhones follow it, and switching to Only me removes the summary at once. Delete my multiplayer profile removes your skills with your profile.

On Android, skills work the same way, with the same three choices. The summary is kept in your multiplayer profile in Firebase instead of iCloud. If you haven't signed in to Play Games and opened Multiplayer or picked a nickname for the leaderboards, you have no profile and your skills stay on your phone.

Once a day we read the summaries shared under Everyone and Friends, on iPhone and on Android, to work out where scores stand among players, so 49 can tell you, for example, that you're ahead of 72% of players. We save only the totals (how many players have each skill, and the score at each percentile), never anyone's own scores. We rely on Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract) for showing your skills as you chose, and on our legitimate interest in improving the game (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) for the totals.

Anonymous usage statistics

To understand how 49 is played and make it better, the iPhone app sends anonymous usage statistics to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service based in Germany. For example: that a game was finished, with its mode, board, time, mistakes and hints, which screens are opened, how far new players get through the welcome steps, and which features (such as the Daily Challenge, hints or multiplayer) are used. Each event carries an anonymous identifier that is hashed on your iPhone before it is sent, so it can't be linked to you, your name, your Apple Account or your device's advertising identifier. Nothing is used for advertising or shared with others. The Android app doesn't use TelemetryDeck.

We rely on our legitimate interest in improving the game (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can turn this off at any time in the app's Settings under "Share anonymous statistics". See TelemetryDeck's privacy policy.

From version 1.0 (38), 49 for iPhone also keeps its own daily counts in Apple's iCloud, in the same public database as multiplayer. Once a day, your iPhone saves how many games you finished and of which kinds, how long 49 was open, whether you played the Daily Challenge, how far you got through the welcome steps, which screens you opened, which of 49's notifications you tapped and whether notifications and the streak reminder are on. These counts carry a random number made only for them, not your nickname, Game Center or player ID, and other players can't read them. As with any iCloud record, Apple notes which iCloud account for 49 saved them: the same ID it notes on a multiplayer profile, which tells us nothing about your name or Apple Account. We don't use it to connect your counts to your profile. They're only saved while "Share anonymous statistics" is on and you're signed in to iCloud. From version 1.0 (39), the counts also include at which hours of the day you open 49, how many days passed since you last played, your streak and whether it ended, streak freezes used, quests finished, your level, whether you met the Daily's goal and took a podium place, how many friends you have in 49, and whether Game Center, iCloud, sound and haptics are on. Still only counts, under the same random number. Turning it off deletes the counts your iPhone saved. The legal basis is the same legitimate interest in improving the game.

49 for Android doesn't send these daily counts.

Waiting list

If you joined the waiting list on this website, your email address and the language of the page you signed up on were sent through Web3Forms, a form service, to our support inbox (contact49game@icloud.com, hosted by Apple's iCloud Mail). We use them only to send you an invite to the TestFlight beta and news about the launch of 49, we never share them, and we delete them when you ask. We rely on your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any time by emailing us. See Web3Forms' privacy policy.

Game Center (optional, iPhone)

If you sign in to Game Center, your scores and achievements are sent to Apple to show leaderboards. 49 also reads the leaderboards to tell you when someone takes your top 3 place, and uses your Game Center name, photo and player ID for multiplayer, as described above. Apple handles Game Center data under its own privacy policy. You can play without Game Center. On Android, 49 uses its own leaderboards, described under Multiplayer and leaderboards on Android.

Google Play Games (Android)

On Android, signing in to Google Play Games is needed for multiplayer, friends and the leaderboards. Google keeps your Play Games profile under its own privacy policy. 49 receives your Play Games player ID and gamer tag: the ID links your sign-in to your Play Games account, and the gamer tag is shown beside your nickname, or as your name until you pick one. 49 doesn't use Play Games achievements, leaderboards or friends lists. You can manage or delete your Play Games profile in the Google Play Games app or in your Google Account. See Google's privacy policy.

Purchases

On iPhone, Premium is sold through the App Store. Apple processes your payment, and we never see your payment details. The app only receives confirmation of what you've bought, so it can turn Premium on.

On Android, Premium is sold through Google Play. Google processes your payment, and we never see your payment details. 49 asks the Play Store app on your phone which plans your Google account owns, so it can turn Premium on, and keeps only whether Premium is on. Nothing about your purchases is sent to us. A Premium bought on iPhone doesn't carry over to Android, and one bought on Android doesn't carry over to iPhone. See Google's privacy policy.

Advertising

On iPhone, the free version shows ads between games, provided by Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, AdMob may collect device identifiers, your approximate location (from your IP address) and information about how you interact with ads. In the EU, the EEA and the UK you are asked for your consent first, and you can change your choice at any time in the app's Settings. 49 never asks to track you across other apps and websites, so Google can't use your device's advertising identifier and the ads aren't personalized to you. See Google's privacy policy.

On Android, the free version also shows ads between games, provided by Google AdMob, and you can choose to watch a video ad for a reward: 30 minutes without ads, new hints, a streak freeze or help bringing back a streak that ended. Ads start once you've played a couple of games. To show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, AdMob collects and shares your phone's advertising ID and other device identifiers (such as the app set ID), your approximate location (from your IP address), how you use the app and its ads (for example app launches, taps and video views) and diagnostic information about the app and the ads. In the EU, the EEA and the UK, Google's consent form asks for your choice before any ad is requested, and you can change it at any time in Settings → About → Ad privacy choices. Your choice is saved on your phone. Google may use your advertising ID to personalize ads unless you refuse in the consent form, or delete your advertising ID or turn off ads personalization in your phone's settings. On phones with Android's Privacy Sandbox, AdMob may also use its ad topics and ad measurement, which you can turn off in your phone's settings under Privacy, Ads.

Premium removes all ads, and with them this data collection, on iPhone and on Android. On Android it takes effect fully the next time you open 49.

Notifications

The streak and Daily reminders, the alert when someone takes your top 3 place on a leaderboard and the alert when quick pairing finds you a game are all scheduled on your iPhone itself. Nothing is sent to us. 49 may also ask iCloud to let your iPhone know about new friend requests, invites and messages.

On Android, the streak reminder and the alert when someone takes your top 3 place are worked out on your phone too. For the alert, your phone reads the leaderboards from Firebase. For friend requests, invites, messages and Daily Challenge alerts, 49 saves your phone's push address (a token from Firebase Cloud Messaging) and your phone's language and region setting in Firebase, where other players can't see them and only our push relay uses them. When a friend sends you something, their phone sends the relay their Firebase sign-in, so it can check the message is real, and the relay asks Firebase Cloud Messaging to tell your phone who sent it and what kind of message it is (with the room's code for an invite), never its text. Your phone then shows the notification in your language. The relay is a small program of ours that runs on Cloudflare's servers, with Cloudflare as our processor. It reads the message and your token only to send that one notification, and it keeps nothing. Deleting your multiplayer profile deletes the token. Firebase may later give your phone a new one, which 49 saves the same way, without a profile.

When another player beats your time in today's Daily Challenge, their phone can wake yours, so 49 checks the board at once and tells you who got ahead of you. The wake-up says only which day to check and is addressed to your player ID, never with a name or a time. On iPhone it goes through iCloud: their iPhone leaves a short note for your 49 profile in iCloud's public database, deleted within a minute. On Android it goes through our push relay, which first checks on the leaderboard that their time really beats yours.

Problem reports

After a crash, or when you tap Report a problem in Settings, 49 offers to email us the diagnostics your iPhone saved: the app and iOS versions, your iPhone model and technical details of the crash or hang. Nothing is sent unless you choose to send it. If you allow sharing analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may also share crash reports with us.

On Android, Contact us under Settings → About opens an email to us with the app and Android versions filled in, and nothing is sent unless you send it. If your phone shares usage and diagnostics with Google, Google Play may share crash reports with us.

Children

49 is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their personal data.

Support

If you contact us, we use your message to help you and keep the conversation only as long as it is useful for that.

This website

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log technical data such as your IP address to keep the service secure, as described in the GitHub privacy statement. The website sets no cookies and loads nothing from third parties. When you pick a language, your browser keeps that choice in its local storage on your device, so the site opens in it next time. It is never sent anywhere. It uses no analytics, with one exception: the page that the QR code on our posters opens (49game.github.io/poster) tells TelemetryDeck that it was opened and on which kind of phone (iPhone, Android or other), then forwards you to the App Store, or on Android to the steps for joining the Android test. Arriving from the poster, a tap on one of those steps tells TelemetryDeck which step it was. Each message carries a random number made for that one visit, nothing about you or your device, and no cookie is set. It only lets us count how many people the posters bring. The site remembers the language you pick in your own browser only, and nothing is sent to us.

Transfers outside the EU

Apple, Google, Cloudflare, GitHub and Web3Forms may process data outside the European Economic Area. On Android, 49's records stay in the EU (Warsaw, and Belgium for live Steal matches), but Firebase's sign-in runs in the United States, and Firebase Cloud Messaging and Cloudflare work worldwide. They rely on safeguards recognised under the GDPR, such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses or the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

Your rights

You can ask to access, correct, delete or export your data, including your multiplayer profile, to restrict or object to how it is used, and to withdraw consent at any time. Just email contact49game@icloud.com. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority. In Poland that is the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO).
